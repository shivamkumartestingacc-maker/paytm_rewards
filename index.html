Product design case study · Paytm UPI rewards

Making UPI rewards worth earning.

A concept exploring how Paytm could make everyday payment rewards more relevant—by helping people see their progress and choose a reward when they qualify.

01 · Why this problem

A reward should give people a reason to come back.

UPI apps can feel interchangeable at the moment of payment. Rewards are one way a payment product can make that everyday action feel more worthwhile—but only if the reward itself feels useful.

The product goal is repeat payments, not just getting someone to open a scratch card. The concept explores whether a clearer path to a reward people value could support that goal.

02 · The problem

When rewards feel irrelevant, people stop looking.

In desk research and informal conversations, people described losing interest when offers felt unpredictable or unlikely to be useful.

Frequent UPI users disengage from rewards when offers feel irrelevant and outcomes unpredictable, leaving little reason to keep checking or earning them.
01

Irrelevant offers lose their appeal

When many rewards are for brands or products someone would not use, the next offer is expected to be the same.

02

People stop checking

Participants described moving from opening every card to ignoring rewards altogether.

03

Uncertain value feels like a gamble

Not knowing what a card contains can make the effort feel less worthwhile than a predictable benefit.

04

Useful value matters more than novelty

Cashback and transparent benefits came up as options people could understand and imagine using.

These are participant-reported experiences, not an audit of Paytm’s product or a measured estimate of user impact.

03 · Earlier flow → proposed flow

Move from “pay and hope” to progress with a choice.

The design direction changes the reward from an unexpected card into an opportunity people can see themselves working toward.

Earlier experience · as described

Pay, then hope

  1. 1Make a UPI payment.
  2. 2Receive an assigned scratch card.
  3. 3Reveal an offer and decide if it is useful.
  4. 4Often leave an irrelevant reward unused.

Proposed flow · concept

Pay, see progress, choose

  1. 1Make an eligible UPI payment.
  2. 2See progress toward a reward pick.
  3. 3Unlock and choose from available rewards.
  4. 4See the benefit and terms, then claim or redeem.
Expected impact · hypothesis, not a result

Visible progress and more relevant choices may make rewards feel worth engaging with, and give people a reason to keep paying with Paytm. The concept has not been tested to show that repeat payments increase.

05 · If this shipped

What I’d measure.

A measurement plan, not results. No baselines, targets or outcomes are claimed.

Proposed primary metric

Repeat Payment Rate

Share of eligible users who make another Paytm UPI payment within a defined window. The population and window would be set before an experiment.

Reward journey

  • Users reaching reward eligibility
  • Reward-selection completion
  • Rewards claimed and, where measurable, redeemed
  • Drop-off between payment and selection

Payment guardrails

  • Payment completion and failure rates
  • Time to complete a payment
  • Reward-related complaints or confusion

Business question

  • Incentive cost per incremental repeat payment, if cost and experiment data become available

06 · Reflection

What this work made clear.

Choice only helps if the options matter.

A choice among irrelevant offers can still feel like no choice at all. The catalogue matters as much as the mechanic.

Progress makes the path legible.

“Three more eligible payments” is more actionable than waiting for a card with an unknown outcome.

A prototype proves a flow, not retention.

The interaction can be explored end to end. Only real testing can tell whether it changes behaviour.

Important questions remain.

Reward economics, eligibility abuse, catalogue relevance and the right threshold all need further investigation.

Part two · Research & process

How the concept came together.

The case study tells the product story. This section follows the evidence trail—from the research approach, through notes and synthesis, to the design exploration. It is exploratory work, not validation.

What’s available in this version: desk research, summaries from five informal conversations, a small set of notes and clusters, JTBD framing, design directions and annotated prototype decisions. The conversations were not formal interviews or a representative study; there are no verbatim transcripts in the current material.

R1 · Conversations

Listening before designing.

The research combined desk research with informal conversations with five people. Questions came up in normal conversation rather than a formal interview.

These are summaries from the existing project documentation, not full interview transcripts.

R2 · Notes → patterns

From individual comments to three themes.

The notes were grouped into patterns. These are interpretations of a small qualitative sample—not prevalence claims about all Paytm users.

Cluster A

Rewards stopped feeling like rewards

Repeated irrelevant outcomes can make checking feel not worth the attention.

Opportunity: Help rebuild confidence that rewards are worth engaging with.

Cluster B

Known value feels safer

Cashback and transparent benefits were preferred because people could understand the value.

Opportunity: Make potential value and conditions easier to understand.

Cluster C

Control may restore motivation

People may be more willing to work toward a reward when they understand what they are working toward.

Opportunity: Let people make an informed choice and see what they may receive.

R3 · Jobs to be done

The progress mechanic is in service of a bigger job.

Functional

“When I make digital payments, I want to build progress toward rewards that are valuable and relevant to me, so my everyday transactions feel meaningful instead of dependent on chance.”

Emotional

“I want to feel recognised for my everyday actions and know that my payments are helping me achieve something I actually value.”

Social

“I want to use a payment app that consistently provides meaningful rewards, so it becomes my preferred payment experience.”

Problem framing · How might we

How might we help frequent UPI users stay engaged with rewards by making every payment contribute toward rewards they value?

R4 · Directions explored

Three ways to make rewards feel more intentional.

Direction 01

Choose a reward

After a payment, choose one of three cards instead of being assigned a random reward.

Direction 02

Choose a category

Pick a reward category, then receive a card from that category after a later payment.

Direction 03

Progress toward a choice

Eligible payments build visible progress toward a reward opportunity.

Direction prototyped: combine visible payment progress with a choice of reward at qualification. These are design directions, not tested alternatives with a proven winner.

R5 · Design decisions

How the research themes shaped the interactions.

The existing prototype annotations document these decisions; the screens themselves will be revisited in the next design pass.

Show progress on Home

Keep the goal visible, including how many eligible payments remain.

Explain eligibility before payment

Make it clear whether a payment counts before the user commits.

Offer a choice after qualifying

Preserve the promise by offering a choice only when the reward is unlocked.

Separate the card from its value

Distinguish the reference value on a card from the configured benefit revealed later.

Make the reveal communicate value

State the amount, whether it is partial or full, and key conditions clearly.

Make the next step obvious

Keep claim, redemption and the return to Home easy to understand.

R6 · How it was built

A browser prototype with working reward logic.

Shared visual system

Colour and type tokens, spacing, buttons, progress segments, reward cards and device frames share CSS components.

Stateful prototype

A single reducer tracks progress, payments, reward selection, reveal and redemption. All values are demo configuration.

Simulated walkthrough

Watch a scripted journey or take over and explore interactions. It has not been tested on physical phones.
